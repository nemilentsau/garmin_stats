# Two-Week Core & Supporting Muscle Program
## Day-by-Day Specification | Phase 1 Foundation
### Periodized for Sub-3:00 Marathon Running

---

## Overview

A 14-day introductory program for building core stability, anti-rotation strength, and supporting muscle resilience for distance running. Periodized around marathon training load. Core rotation (A-B-C-D) advances daily. Supporting muscle work appears only on easy/rest days.

**Core Philosophy:** Every exercise serves running mechanics. No cosmetic ab work. Anti-movement > movement. Fatigue-resistant stability > raw strength.

**Prerequisites:** Access to a gym with cables, pull-up bar, ab wheel, med ball, resistance bands. Bodyweight alternatives provided for travel days.

---

## Exercise Reference

### Core Rotation Exercises

#### Day A — Rotation Focus

| ID | Exercise | Target | Cues |
|----|----------|--------|------|
| A1 | Cable rotations (horizontal) | Obliques, transverse abdominis | Hips locked forward. Initiate from obliques, not arms. Control the return — the eccentric is where the running transfer lives. |
| A2 | Pallof press + overhead reach | Anti-rotation, shoulder-core integration | Press out, then reach overhead. The overhead component adds anti-extension demand. If you feel your ribs flare, you've gone too far. |
| A3 | Dead bugs | Contralateral coordination, anti-extension | Press low back into floor. Extend opposite arm/leg WITHOUT letting back arch. This is literally the running pattern — opposite arm, opposite leg. |
| A4 | Plank shoulder taps | Anti-rotation under dynamic load | Narrow your feet for more challenge. Hips should not rock side to side — if they do, widen stance or slow down. |

#### Day B — Anti-Extension Focus

| ID | Exercise | Target | Cues |
|----|----------|--------|------|
| B1 | Ab wheel rollouts | Anti-extension, full anterior chain | Start from knees. Roll out only as far as you can return without your back sagging. The moment your low back dips = too far. Quality > range. |
| B2 | Hanging leg raises | Hip flexor strength, lower abs, grip | Control the descent — no swinging. Initiate by tilting the pelvis (posterior tilt), not by yanking the knees. Decompresses the spine after lifting. |
| B3 | Body saw plank (sliders) | Anti-extension under movement | Forearm plank with feet on sliders. Rock forward/backward. Tiny range — 3-4 inches. Your core fights extension as your lever arm changes. |
| B4 | Hollow body hold | Global anterior tension, anti-extension | Arms overhead, legs straight, low back glued to floor. If your back lifts, bend knees or lower arms to sides. This is the position your core holds at mile 22. |

#### Day C — Oblique & Lateral Focus

| ID | Exercise | Target | Cues |
|----|----------|--------|------|
| C1 | Cable rotations (high-to-low woodchop) | Obliques through diagonal plane | Pull from high to opposite hip. The diagonal vector mimics the rotational forces during running turns and uneven terrain. |
| C2 | Copenhagen plank | Adductors + lateral core | Top foot on bench, bottom leg hangs. Hold. If too hard, bend the top knee on the bench. Adductor strength prevents groin issues — one of the most common runner injuries. |
| C3 | Suitcase carry | Anti-lateral flexion, grip, obliques | Heavy dumbbell in one hand, walk tall. Don't lean away from the weight — your obliques fight to keep you upright. This is what your core does every stride (single-leg stance = asymmetric load). |
| C4 | Side plank + hip dip | Obliques, glute medius, lateral stability | Side plank, drop hip toward floor, drive back up. Controlled tempo — 2s down, 1s up. The dip challenges the exact muscles that prevent pelvic drop while running. |

#### Day D — Dynamic / Power

| ID | Exercise | Target | Cues |
|----|----------|--------|------|
| D1 | Cable rotations (low-to-high) | Obliques through ascending diagonal | Pull from low to opposite shoulder. Feet stay planted — all rotation comes from the trunk. Completes the three cable rotation vectors (horizontal, high-to-low, low-to-high). |
| D2 | Hanging windshield wipers | Rotational control under load | Hang from bar, legs up, rotate side to side. Start with bent knees if straight legs are too much. This is the most advanced rotational exercise in the program. |
| D3 | Med ball slams | Power, rate of force development | Overhead, slam into floor. Full hip extension at the top, aggressive hip flexion on the way down. This is the only explosive exercise in the rotation — it trains power output, not endurance. |
| D4 | Turkish get-up | Full-body stability, shoulder integration | Slow and deliberate. Each position is a checkpoint — pause for 1-2s at every stage. This exercise exposes every stability weakness you have. |

### Supporting Muscle Exercises

#### Block 1 — Glutes & Hip Stability

| ID | Exercise | Target | Cues |
|----|----------|--------|------|
| G1 | Banded clamshells | Glute medius, external rotators | Side-lying, knees at 45°. Open top knee against band. Pause 2s at top. Don't let hips roll backward. |
| G2 | Single-leg glute bridge | Glute max, unilateral hip extension | Drive through heel. Full hip extension at top — squeeze glute hard. If hamstring cramps, your glute isn't firing — reset. |
| G3 | Lateral band walks | Glute medius, hip abductors | Quarter squat, band above knees. Step sideways without feet touching. Stay low — don't pop up between steps. |
| G4 | Banded marching | Hip flexors, core integration | Stand tall, band around feet. Drive one knee up against resistance. This strengthens the exact hip flexion pattern used in running leg turnover. |

#### Block 2 — Lower Leg & Foot

| ID | Exercise | Target | Cues |
|----|----------|--------|------|
| L1 | Heavy slow calf raises (straight leg) | Gastrocnemius, Achilles tendon stiffness | 3s up, 3s down. Full range — drop heel below the step, rise to full plantarflexion. Load progressively. |
| L2 | Bent-knee calf raises (soleus) | Soleus — primary propulsive muscle in distance running | Same tempo. Bent knee shifts load from gastrocnemius to soleus. This muscle fatigues first on long runs. |
| L3 | Tib raises | Tibialis anterior, shin splint prevention | Stand on heels, lift toes toward shins. Simple, boring, prevents shin splints. 2 minutes of your day. |
| L4 | Short foot / single-leg balance | Intrinsic foot muscles, arch stability | Stand on one leg, scrunch the arch without curling toes. Hold 30s. If it wobbles, that's the training stimulus. |

#### Block 3 — Eccentric / Reactive Stiffness

| ID | Exercise | Target | Cues |
|----|----------|--------|------|
| E1 | Nordic hamstring curls | Eccentric hamstring strength | Kneel, feet anchored, lower yourself as slowly as possible. Catch with hands, push back up. Even partial range counts — the eccentric is everything. |
| E2 | Single-leg RDL | Hip hinge stability, hamstring eccentric, balance | Hinge forward on one leg, free leg extends back. Hips SQUARE to the floor. Touch the ground if mobility allows. This is the king of runner-specific single-leg work. |
| E3 | Standing knee drives (banded) | Hip flexor power through range | Band anchored behind, drive knee up against resistance. Fast and controlled. Builds the power component of leg turnover. |
| E4 | Adductor squeeze | Adductor strength, groin injury prevention | Copenhagen lite (easier than C2) or ball squeeze between knees. Adductors contribute 20-30% of running propulsion — don't neglect them. |

## Week 1: Build the Rotation

**Goal:** Learn all 4 core days at reduced volume. Introduce 2 of 3 supporting blocks. Establish the habit of daily core work. Learn the RPE 6-7 intensity zone — never to failure.

**Training week assumed:** Mon intervals, Tue easy, Wed tempo, Thu easy, Fri rest, Sat long run, Sun easy.

---

### Day 1 — Monday (Hard: Intervals) — Core Day A

| Slot | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|-------|
| A1 | Cable rotations (horizontal) | 2×8 each side | First exposure. Start with light weight — find the movement pattern. The cable should feel like it's pulling you into rotation; your obliques resist and then reverse it. |
| A2 | Pallof press + overhead reach | 2×6 each side | Skip the overhead reach today — just do standard Pallof press (press out, hold 2s, return). Learn the base pattern first. |
| A3 | Dead bugs | 2×8 each side | Slow. Press low back into floor before you start moving limbs. If your back arches, reduce range of motion on the extending leg. |

**Volume:** 3 exercises, 2 sets each. ~8 min total. Hard day = reduced dose, skip Day A's 4th exercise (plank shoulder taps).

**Timing:** Do this after morning breathwork, before your run. Think of it as activation, not training. RPE 5-6.

---

### Day 2 — Tuesday (Easy Run) — Core Day B + Supporting Block 1

| Slot | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|-------|
| **Core B** | | | |
| B1 | Ab wheel rollouts | 2×8 | First exposure. From knees. Don't go to full extension — find the point where your core has to fight hard, stop there, and return. That's your current range. |
| B2 | Hanging leg raises | 2×10 | Start with knee raises if straight-leg is too hard. The key is the pelvic tilt at the bottom of each rep — tuck your pelvis before lifting. |
| B3 | Body saw plank | 2×6 | First exposure. Tiny range of motion. You'll feel this in places you didn't know existed. |
| B4 | Hollow body hold | 2×20s | Start with arms by your sides (easier) rather than overhead. If your back lifts off the floor, bend your knees. |
| **Supporting Block 1** | | | |
| G1 | Banded clamshells | 2×12 each side | Light band. Slow and controlled. Pause at top. |
| G2 | Single-leg glute bridge | 2×10 each side | Drive through heel. If hamstring cramps, tap the glute a few times to wake it up (seriously, this works). |
| G3 | Lateral band walks | 2×10 each direction | Stay low in quarter squat. Don't let the trailing foot snap to the leading foot. |
| G4 | Banded marching | 2×8 each side | First exposure. The band tension should challenge you to maintain upright posture — don't lean back. |

**Volume:** 4 core exercises + 4 supporting exercises, 2 sets each. ~25 min total. Easy day = full dose.

**First time doing core + supporting in the same morning.** The supporting work will feel easy — it is. These are activation-level loads building motor patterns, not max-effort lifts.

### Day 3 — Wednesday (Hard: Tempo) — Core Day C

| Slot | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|-------|
| C1 | Cable rotations (high-to-low) | 2×8 each side | First exposure to the diagonal pattern. Same weight as Day 1 horizontal rotations. The vector change makes it feel different even at the same load. |
| C2 | Copenhagen plank | 2×15s each side | First exposure. This may be surprisingly hard. Bend the top knee on the bench if full extension is too much. If you can't hold 15s, hold what you can — even 8s counts. |
| C3 | Side plank + hip dip | 2×8 each side | Skip suitcase carry today (saving it for Week 2). Side plank dips are the priority — they directly train the pelvic drop resistance you need while running. |

**Volume:** 3 exercises, 2 sets each. ~8 min total. Hard day = reduced, skip C3 (suitcase carry).

**Note on Copenhagen plank:** If you've never done this, it will expose adductor weakness you didn't know you had. Soreness the next day is normal. This is the exercise most likely to prevent groin strains during marathon training.

### Day 4 — Thursday (Easy Run) — Core Day D + Supporting Block 2

| Slot | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|-------|
| **Core D** | | | |
| D1 | Cable rotations (low-to-high) | 2×8 each side | Third cable rotation vector. By now you should feel how the three angles (horizontal, high-to-low, low-to-high) hit different parts of the obliques. |
| D2 | Hanging windshield wipers | 2×6 | First exposure. Bent knees to start. If you can't control the rotation, do hanging knee twists (smaller range) instead. This is the hardest exercise in the rotation — give it time. |
| D3 | Med ball slams | 2×8 | First exposure. Pick a weight you can slam aggressively 8 times. This is the only exercise where you're supposed to be explosive — everything else is controlled. |
| D4 | Turkish get-up | 2×2 each side | First exposure. Use no weight or a very light dumbbell. Go through each position slowly — there are 7 distinct checkpoints in a full TGU. Learn the positions before adding load. |
| **Supporting Block 2** | | | |
| L1 | Heavy slow calf raises (straight leg) | 2×8 | Bodyweight only for Week 1. 3s up, 3s down. Full range on a step. |
| L2 | Bent-knee calf raises | 2×8 | Same tempo. Feel the difference — bent knee isolates the soleus. |
| L3 | Tib raises | 2×12 | Stand on heels, lift toes. Boring but important. |
| L4 | Short foot / single-leg balance | 2×20s each side | Just stand on one leg. Don't close your eyes yet — that's a Week 2+ progression. |

**Volume:** 4 core exercises + 4 supporting exercises, 2 sets each. ~28 min total.

**TGU note:** If you've never done a Turkish get-up, watch one video of the full movement before attempting it. The positions matter more than the weight. Many people spend 2-3 sessions just learning the pattern with zero load.

|------|----------|-------------|-------|

| A4 | Plank shoulder taps | 2×10 each side | **First time including the 4th exercise.** Rest day = full dose. Shoulder-width feet to start. Narrow as it gets easier. |
| **Supporting Block 1** | | | |
| G1 | Banded clamshells | 2×15 each side | Increase reps from Day 2. Add 2s pause at top. |
| G2 | Single-leg glute bridge | 2×12 each side | Increase reps. Is one side noticeably weaker? Add 3 extra reps on that side. |
| G3 | Lateral band walks | 2×12 each direction | Increase reps. |
| G4 | Banded marching | 2×10 each side | Increase reps. |
| **Supporting Block 2** | | | |
| L1 | Heavy slow calf raises | 2×8 | Same as Day 4. Focus on full range and tempo. |
| L2 | Bent-knee calf raises | 2×8 | Same. |
| L3 | Tib raises | 2×15 | Increase reps. |
| L4 | Short foot / single-leg balance | 2×25s each side | Increase hold time by 5s. |

**Volume:** 4 core exercises + 8 supporting exercises, ~35 min total. Rest day = biggest session of the week.

**This is the first time you do both supporting blocks in one session.** It's a lot of exercises but each is low-intensity. Think of it as a prehab circuit, not a workout.

### Day 6 — Saturday (Hard: Long Run) — Core Day B

| Slot | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|-------|
| B1 | Ab wheel rollouts | 2×8 | Second exposure. Has your range increased since Day 2? Even an inch counts. |
| B2 | Hanging leg raises | 2×10 | If you did knee raises on Day 2, try straight-leg today for at least the first set. |
| B3 | Body saw plank | 2×6 | Same range. Focus on breathing during the movement — exhale as you saw forward (hardest position). |

**Volume:** 3 exercises, 2 sets each. ~8 min total. Hard day = reduced, skip B4 (hollow body hold).

**Pre-long-run note:** This is activation only. Don't push intensity. You want your core "switched on" for the long run, not fatigued.

### Day 7 — Sunday (Easy Run) — Core Day C + Supporting Block 3

| Slot | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|-------|
| **Core C** | | | |
| C1 | Cable rotations (high-to-low) | 2×10 each side | Increase reps from Day 3. Second exposure to this vector. |
| C2 | Copenhagen plank | 2×15s each side | Same hold time as Day 3. Is it easier? If yes, straighten the top leg fully next week. If still very hard, stay here. |
| C3 | Suitcase carry | 2×20m each side | **First exposure.** Pick a heavy dumbbell (challenging but walkable). Tall posture, don't lean away from the weight. Walk slowly — the slower you go, the harder it is. |
| C4 | Side plank + hip dip | 2×10 each side | Increase reps from Day 3. |
| **Supporting Block 3** | | | |
| E1 | Nordic hamstring curls | 2×3 | **First exposure.** 3 reps is enough. Lower yourself as slowly as possible — even 3 seconds of eccentric counts. Catch with hands, push back up. Don't worry about the concentric. |
| E2 | Single-leg RDL | 2×6 each side | **First exposure.** Bodyweight only. Focus on keeping hips square. Touch the floor if you can, but don't round your back to get there. |
| E3 | Standing knee drives (banded) | 2×8 each side | **First exposure.** Band behind you, drive knee up. Fast but controlled. |
| E4 | Adductor squeeze | 2×8 | Ball between knees, squeeze. Simple. Builds the base for Copenhagen plank progression. |

**Volume:** 4 core + 4 supporting, 2 sets each. ~28 min total.

**Block 3 introduction:** These exercises are the most demanding of the supporting blocks. Nordics in particular will make you sore. Starting at 3 reps on Day 7 (not Day 1) gives your body a week of foundation before introducing eccentric hamstring load.

### Week 1 Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Core sessions | 7 (daily, never skipped) |
| Core rotation cycles | 1.75 (A-B-C-D-A-B-C) |
| Supporting sessions | 3 (Tue, Thu: 1 block each; Fri: 2 blocks; Sun: 1 block) |
| Total core minutes | ~65 min |
| Total supporting minutes | ~45 min |
| Total program minutes | ~110 min |
| Exercises introduced | All 16 core + all 12 supporting = 28 exercises |

**End-of-week check-in:**
1. Which core day feels hardest? (Usually D — windshield wipers and TGU expose weaknesses.)
2. Which supporting exercise surprised you with difficulty? (Usually Copenhagen plank or nordics.)
3. Any left-right asymmetry noticed? (Log it — this is important data for injury prevention.)
4. Are you maintaining RPE 6-7 or grinding to failure? (If the latter, reduce weight/reps.)
5. Any soreness that persists >48 hours? (Normal: obliques after first cable rotations, adductors after Copenhagen. Not normal: sharp joint pain anywhere.)

## What Comes After Day 14

**Weeks 3-4 (full Phase 1 Foundation):**
- All core exercises at 3 sets on easy/rest days
- Add load: cable rotations +5 lbs, carries +10 lbs, TGU +5 lbs
- Supporting work solidified at 3-4 sessions/week
- Nordics progress to 3×5
- Calf raises add meaningful load (25+ lbs)
- Begin tracking pace:HR ratio correlation with core compliance

**Weeks 5-8 (Phase 2 Extension):**
- Ab wheel: progress from knees toward standing
- Hanging leg raises: progress to L-sit holds or ankle weights
- Copenhagen plank: add top-leg hip flexion/extension
- Dead bugs: add band or dumbbell
- Hollow body: progress to hollow rocks
- Soleus calf raises: 5-second eccentrics
- Nordics: full range without band assist
- Glute medius: floor work → standing cable abduction

**Weeks 9+ (Phase 3 Optimization):**
- Pallof press with controlled rotation
- Replace hollow body with full hollow rocks
- Standing ab wheel rollouts
- Loaded single-leg RDL (heavy)
- Introduce single-leg squat progressions (pistol/skater)
- Periodize core by mesocycle: accumulation (volume) → intensification (load) → deload

### Session Timer Specifications

**Timed holds (Copenhagen, side plank, hollow body, balance):**
- Countdown display with large numbers
- Color shift at 75% of target time (acknowledges difficulty without alarming)
- Completion tone

**Carries (suitcase carry):**
- Distance estimate based on time (assume 2 ft/second walking pace)
- "Turn around" prompt at halfway

**Med ball slams:**
- Rep counter (tap to count)
- No timer needed — power exercises shouldn't be rushed

**Turkish get-up:**
- Checkpoint mode: shows current position (1 of 7), tap to advance
- Optional form cue at each checkpoint
- No timer — TGU is never about speed

**All exercises:**
- Rest timer between sets (60s default, adjustable)
- "Next exercise" prompt after final set
- Swap button (shows variation alternatives)
